## What was built

### Registry (`crates/registry`, `crates/registry-macros`; ADR-0013 §1, §10, §13)

**Declaring commands.** `#[command(...)]` sits on a handler `fn(&mut Cx, Params) -> Result<Out, CommandError>`. It:
- declares id, kind, label, scopes, undo label, confirmation conditions, enablement rule, project requirement,
  examples (required), GUI location and `agentOnly`;
- derives the parameter and result schemas from the types.

**What is registered.** `Registry::assemble` combines:
- the spec A inventory: 796 entries, generated by `scripts/gen-command-inventory.py` and kept equal to spec A by
  `crates/registry/tests/inventory.rs`;
- the typed declarations: 134. Of these, 62 are implemented and 72 are declared with their owning checkpoint.

**Behaviour of entries without an implementation.**
- Stubs and declared-later commands validate their parameters, then fail with `command.notImplemented
  {commandId, milestone}`.
- Events fail with `command.disabled`.

**Registry version** is `0.1.0`.

**Drawing commands.** The 02 and 04 drawing commands are recorded as delegations (`DELEGATES`). They are not aliases.

### Executor (`crates/executor`; ADR-0013 §2–§8, §11–§13)

**Document thread and handle.** `ExecutorHandle` provides:
- `spawn`, `invoke`, `batch`, `open_document`, `snapshot`, `subscribe`;
- `list_commands`, which evaluates enablement per origin;
- `describe_command`;
- `jobs`.

One document thread owns the session. A watchdog bounds every call (`command.timeout`). `job.wait` runs off the
document thread.

**Mutation capability (`Cx`).**
- `Cx` is the only way to stage a change: `patch_objects` or `patch_entries`.
- It also resolves references, including special references.
- It carries warnings and targets.

**Transactions.**
- Commits go through `Document::with_patched_entries_undo_only`, so the full C1.1 staged integrity guarantee is kept.
- Change records come from touched-object own views.
- Revision stamping includes drawing content.
- An edit that changes nothing does not commit.

**Undo, redo and history.**
- Undo and redo are transactions. They replay with verified preconditions (`history.diverged`) and keep the redo that
  undo returns.
- `edit.history.move` is supported.
- Step ids are stable across replays.
- The history holds 1 000 steps and a clean marker.

**Other capabilities.**
- Copy-on-write checkpoints.
- `doc.diff`, rebuilt from the revision log.
- Atomic and sequential batches with `$n`/`$name` references.
- `dryRun` (both the option and the parameter), `requestId`, `expectRev`.
- Events: `doc.changed`, `edit.history.changed`, `app.state.changed`, `edit.selection.changed`, `workspace.changed`,
  `project.dirty.changed`, `project.opened`, `edit.clipboard.changed`, `job.progress`, `job.finished`.

## Final C1.1 tickets owned by C1.2 — status

### Executor builder: commit through the staged commit; canonical undo and redo; stateless inversion stays removed

**Done.** Commits go through `Document::with_patched_entries[_undo_only]`, the multi-entry form of
`with_patched_entry`, which delegates to it. Exact canonical patches, numeric object keys and concrete paths are
preserved: the stored undo is the verified canonical diff of C11-R6-01.

The redo used is the one returned by undo. A replay applies the stored patch and keeps the verified patch it
returns as the other direction. Before applying, every precondition is checked against the live entry modulo `rev`.

There is no stateless inversion.

- `c12_transactions.rs::undo_and_redo_replay_verified_patches_and_keep_step_ids`, which runs three undo/redo cycles
  and checks the exact state each time;
