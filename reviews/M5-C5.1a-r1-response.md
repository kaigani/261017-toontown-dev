`crates/executor/src/timing_gesture.rs`, `run` (release) and `outcome`; the kernel note §11 states it for every gesture
kind. At the release the plan is made **once, against the press-time snapshot**, so its targets are the ids resolved at
the press and the content it grabbed is the snapshot's; the press position is never hit-tested again. The plan is then
resolved to its outcome in absolute values against the snapshot and against the present document, for the same ids:

cell move's destination, the drawings and notes of the grabbed cells and the destination cells; and, for every command,
the ids it names that no longer exist. Equal outcomes commit the plan; different outcomes cancel with

## 2. What the rulings asked besides the fixes

### 2.1 What the executor does when a handler panics (E-21)

A handler panic ends the document thread: the call in flight answers `internal` "the document thread has stopped" at
once, every later call answers the same, the open session is lost with the thread (what is on disk and in the last
autosave's recovery data is kept), and nothing restarts inside the executor. In the desktop host the restart banner
does **not** appear after a panic (the watchdog counts neither a miss nor an answer for a heartbeat answered with
`internal`), so the person sees every action fail until they quit. Test: `crates/executor/tests/c51_handler_panic.rs`
(its own binary: the hook it sets is process-wide). The full account, and the assessment of containment, is
`reviews/M5-design-handler-panics.md` (E-26 item 4); nothing of it is built.
