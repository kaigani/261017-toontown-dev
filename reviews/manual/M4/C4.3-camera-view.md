# C4.3 — The Camera view against a final render (≈ 8 min)

Spec: 05 §3.2 (the Camera view), §1.2 (the camera frame), §12; 02 §10.5-§10.7 (current drawing on top, selection
display); camera note §6; plan §10.

**Prerequisites** (everything this script assumes; nothing else is needed):

- **A build of the handoff head:** the CI artifact `desktop-Windows` or `desktop-macOS` of the run named in
  `reviews/M4-C4.3b-handoff.md` §1 (Windows: `default-build/toontown.exe`; macOS: unpack
  `Toontown-macos-arm64.app.tar.gz`), or a local `pnpm --dir desktop tauri build --no-bundle`.
- **The fixture project** `reviews/manual/M4/fixtures/c43-camera.animpkg` (Bob, a black rectangle at the centre; Ann,
  a black ellipse to the left and behind him), copied somewhere writable. Open it with File › Open….
- **An agent client** for the steps that name a command: the one in `C4.1-attached-agent.md` (Python 3.11+,
  `mcp==2.0.0`; Preferences › Agent access: **agent.server.enabled** on). Commands are typed as
  `run_command {"id": "…", "params": {…}}`.

1. Open the fixture. The Camera view is central (the default workspace) → Bob and Ann are drawn, Ann partly behind
   Bob; the canvas is sharp at your display scale.
2. Zoom the Camera view out with the wheel until the camera frame is smaller than the view → the area outside the frame
   is dimmed and the frame has a thin outline; Bob and Ann stay where they were inside it.
3. Render the frame: `run_command {"id": "render.frame.run", "params": {"composition": "activeView", "out": "<a path>.png"}}`
   (or File › Export when it exists) and open the PNG next to the window → inside the frame the window and the render
   show the same drawing, the same order, the same proportions.
4. Roll the view: `run_command {"id": "view.rotation.set", "params": {"viewId": "cameraView", "degrees": 20}}` → the
   whole composition, frame included, turns by 20° on screen; the render is unchanged (the camera did not move).
   `view.transform.reset` brings it back.
5. Click Bob with the Select scene tool (`run_command {"id": "anim.tool.set", "params": {"tool": "select"}}`) → his
   box, his pivot cross and the eight scale handles appear; the status bar says one item selected.
6. Turn box highlighting off (the view toolbar's **Box highlighting** button, off) → Ann is drawn faded, Bob at full
   strength. Turn it on and turn **Outline on selection** on → Bob gets an outline in the highlight colour, drawn even
   where Ann covers him.
7. Turn **Current drawing on top** on with Ann's element current
   (`run_command {"id": "app.context.set", "params": {"elementId": "el_01K7JJN80055VQSF7WREM7JZEP", "frame": 1}}`) → Ann is drawn in front
   of Bob in the Camera view; the render (step 3 again) still has Bob in front.
8. Show the Drawing view as well (Window › Drawing view) and make it active by clicking in it, then click in the Camera
   view → the Camera view becomes the active view at that click, and the click also acts (it selects what is under it).
   Nothing appears in the undo list for the activation.
9. **Frame budget on an uncapped display (ruling D-33).** On a 60 Hz display with no frame-rate cap (no driver or
   G-SYNC limit), run `python scripts/c43-camera-measure.py <toontown binary> <out folder> --backend webgl2 --bounds
   workstation` and again with `--backend webgpu` (Python 3.11+, `mcp==2.0.0`, a checkout of the repository) → the
   receipt's `record.control.frameMs.p50` is about 16.7 ms and the frame time checks of plan §6 (17.5 ms p95, both
   scenes) pass. On the builder's workstation the control paced at 17.6 ms (an external cap), so only D-33's work and
   long-interval figures were assessed there.
