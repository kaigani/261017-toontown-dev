The test actually creates eight long render jobs, sends four waits (120 s), holds two server work threads with a hook (120 s release guard), and issues two coalesced roots notifications without pumping client responses during the count. It waits until both work threads enter the hook, then counts before releasing either; all eight jobs are checked Running. mcp reader dispatches waits independently of the serial call worker (:529); work timeout is five seconds but the slots remain taken until their held threads exit (:1235-1258). Roots refresh waits up to 30 s (:660), spanning the roughly five-second work-start wait, and roots_busy coalesces notifications (:619). The next wait/work/job requests assert both limit.exceeded and concurrentJobWaits/serverWork/maxJobs respectively. Together the measured full K, running-job assertion, held-work count and typed refusals establish simultaneous saturation. The test does not individually expose a roots-thread counter; its non-answering client and the roots timeout establish its lifetime at measurement. The two isolated tests serialize via SERIAL.

ServerWork is an enum variant, but the hook storage and execution are cfg(feature = test-hooks), persist/lock.rs: hooks module and boundary. Without that feature boundary only discards its arguments; the product call at mcp/server.rs:1246 cannot wait on a hook. The workspace check enables the feature through executor dev-dependency, explicitly supplied here to reproduce that unification. No product dependency was added.

Heartbeat: recovery.rs heartbeat_owned uses OWNERSHIP.try_lock and returns immediately on contention. Maintenance records its scheduled heartbeat time even when skipped (maintenance.rs:46-50); repeated writes by another engine may overlap arbitrarily many minute ticks, so repeated skips are possible. No fairness bound proves eventual refresh. RecoveryInfo::orphaned (:53) uses age over 300 s OR known process death, so a live engine's completed entry can appear orphaned after enough skips. That is distinct from scavenger deletion: scavenge (:214) skips every folder containing recovery.json, rechecks that file immediately before removal, and for incomplete entries explicitly guards stale with !alive, treating current PID or known live same-host PID as alive. Its age is the owner sidecar modification time, not heartbeatAt. Thus the scavenger leaves a live owner's record untouched even after consecutive skips; a completed recovery is never removed by it. O-18 remains satisfied for this changed path.
