## Timing and determinism interpretation

All interval/fps calculations use consecutive **rAF timestamps within one page**, never subtract a Rust clock or wall clock from them. `timeOrigin`, host environment and wall-clock start/save fields are retained. WebKit's rounded timestamps explain why mean idle interval is the appropriate displayed refresh estimate. Percentiles are nearest-rank; 10 transfer samples make p95 equal max. The measured-frame span is about 9,983–10,000 ms because the loop starts its timer one callback before recording its first sample; this is the declared 10 s experiment with one endpoint interval absent, not a ten-second distribution including load/decode.

`onSubmittedWorkDone` measures **submission-to-completion notification latency**, including outstanding queue work and JS callback scheduling. It is not a GPU timestamp-query duration. WebGL2 reports no GPU duration; `flush` is not completion, while upload uses `finish`. CPU timing starts inside `renderer.frame`: it includes buffer/uniform update, command recording and submission, but excludes `cameraAt`, CPU `animateGroups`, bookkeeping and all load/decode. Frame cadence includes their effect. These measurements support the tested camera/geometry workload, not a complete M4 frame budget.

`1000 / median transfer` is a useful **typical sequential transfer-only estimate**, not a mathematical upper bound on every pipelined architecture. Mean reciprocal service time, tail latency, batching and overlap matter. Neither that caveat nor unmeasured GL GPU time rescues resending 8.7 MB plus 85–100 ms decoding every 16.7 ms. The doubled-buffer test supports practical headroom, while its 18.7 ms worst completion sample cautions against equating a 60 fps mean with every GPU completion under one refresh.

`ttraster` uses ordered vectors/loops, no rayon, HashMap accumulation or `mul_add`; no fast-math flags appear in this repository. Its arithmetic has fixed source ordering and the tested compiler/profile variations preserve hashes. In vello's multithreaded dispatcher, path work carries monotonic task indices through an **ordered channel**; fine rasterization writes disjoint output regions. There is no observed completion-order-dependent floating-point reduction. This structural evidence plus 50 repeats is stronger than a green hash alone, but is not a proof for every future filter/scene.

Production vello flattening explicitly contains SIMD `mul_add` and square-root operations; gradient/coverage evaluation has finite-precision thresholds. Our stress corpus varies their inputs and demonstrates dispatch dependence, without attributing it specifically to FMA or libm. Real transcendental paths are mapped in R1-06. In particular, Gaussian blur, arbitrary filters, round joins/caps and colour-space conversions are not certified by the current adapter's bevel/butt, sRGB-u8 corpus.

**(1) Host-raster bitmap per frame:** this is a valid *second fallback experiment* and a useful reduced-performance/software path, especially because spec 06 §6.9 requires operation without GPU acceleration. It is not a 60 fps fallback at the measured sizes: transfer/upload already exceeds a frame, and three-thread 100 k rasterization adds 196–393 ms. The pattern upload measures transport rather than real end-to-end raster presentation, and the raster benchmark reconstructs/encodes the scene each render. A cached or tiled implementation may improve small edits, but no result here establishes that improvement; retain native wgpu as an estimate, not an implemented escape hatch.

**(2) GPU-resident scene and deltas:** the evidence strongly supports keeping reusable drawing geometry resident and sending transforms, palette/paint changes and bounded dirty geometry, with an overlay for in-progress gestures. It does not yet choose the wire protocol. M4 needs versioned identities, ownership/lifetime, backpressure, invalidation for zoom-dependent flattening and deformations, and a resynchronization path. The final CPU renderer must still consume the same logical render list/content/order as the interactive renderer (spec 06 §6.1); residence is a transport/cache choice, not permission for the two renderers to diverge. The current two-draw-call “all fills then strokes” simplification and one-time flattening remain explicit spike limitations.

**(3) vello_cpu versus ttraster:** neither adapter is ready for spec 09 §4 / 06 §§6–9 adoption without more work. `ttraster` has solid/linear/radial paint, ordinary nested non-zero clips, bevel/butt strokes and 8-bit signed-area coverage, but lacks mirrored/independently transformed gradient coverage, bitmap/vector/alpha textures, inverted and subtree-alpha clips, flatten/effect layers, radial/directional blur, colour transforms/management, production stroke styles and quality/cache modes. Its coverage approximation for overlapping edges and AA seams needs correctness goldens; feather bands need vector/compositor integration. vello supplies a much broader upstream paint/layer/image/filter toolkit, but this adapter tests only a narrow subset: no full spec blur/flatten/inverted-mask pipeline, separate paint transforms, colour-transform order, feather integration, colour management or f32 quality mode is established. Both use premultiplied internal RGBA and sRGB-space blending; a documented straight-alpha input/output conversion is needed, and R1-05 shows it cannot be dismissed as identity. Vello's speed and maintained toolkit are attractive, with permissive licenses and zero unsafe in its own two main crates but substantial transitive implementation/unsafe exposure. Ttraster's 487 dependency-free lines shift all feature, performance and fidelity maintenance to this project. A defensible C0.4 choice must settle SIMD policy and opacity/quality correctness rather than select on 92 matching hashes alone.
