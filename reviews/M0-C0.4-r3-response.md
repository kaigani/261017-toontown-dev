## C04-R3-02 — key auto-repeat — fixed as ruled

`TabletSpike.tsx` handles Space and K like this:
- a transition happens **only on keyup**, and only after a non-repeat keydown of the same key;
- keydowns with `event.repeat` are ignored;
- a transition within **500 ms** of the previous one is ignored;
- both keydown and keyup are still default-prevented, so Space never activates a focused toolbar button;
- typing in the form, and `--spike-auto`, stay excluded.

Every Space/K event goes into the log as `keyEvents: [{type, key, repeat, t, outcome}]`, with outcomes such as
"pressed", "transition: A1 started", "ignored: auto-repeat", "ignored: within 500 ms of the last transition",
"ignored: keyup without its keydown" and "ignored: typing in the form". Other keys are not recorded, since they
carry no meaning and could be typed text.

The analyzer validates `keyEvents`: types, keys, times in order, and transitions only on non-repeat keyups. The
transition count must equal what the action records need (done = 2, skipped = 1, unfinished = 1), otherwise exit 2.
So a corrupted sequence is rejected and diagnosable. Logs without `keyEvents`, such as the reviewer's fixtures, are
still read. The manual tests say to press and release each key once, that holding does nothing, and to click an
empty part of the page first, as the reviewer suggested.

**My tests** (real page, controlled clock, in `actions.test.tsx`; 8 pass):
- held Space with two repeats → only A1 started;
- held K with repeats → only A4 skipped;
- two presses 100 ms apart → one transition;
- a keyup without its keydown → ignored;
- Space on a focused toolbar button → a marker, and neither keydown nor keyup activates the button;
- typing in the form → no marker, four "ignored" events;
- the full six-action procedure;
- tampered key logs (a lost transition, a transition on a keydown) → exit 2.

### Disputed note 1 — three reviewer page tests cannot pass under the ruled mechanism

`tests/adversarial/c04-r3-page.py` runs five tests. Both controls about typing and `--spike-auto` pass. The other
three dispatch **keydown events only**, with no keyup and no time between them:
- "explicit Space on a focused toolbar button marks an action" sends two keydowns and expects A1 done;
- "held Space" sends one keydown and two repeats and expects A1 unfinished;
- "one held K" sends three keydown pairs and a K keydown with repeats, and expects four actions.

Under the ruling ("act on keyup only, require a minimum 500 ms between marker transitions"), no event in those tests
can make a transition, so they record no action and fail. The ruling and those three assertions cannot both hold.
- I implemented the ruling and did not edit the tests.
- The tests' intent is met: a held key never advances more than one action, and toolbar activation is suppressed.
  My tests above show it with real press/release pairs.
- If you rule an amendment, the minimal one is per scenario:
  1. add `fireEvent.keyUp` after each deliberate press;
  2. advance a controlled `performance.now()` at least 500 ms between presses, as `actions.test.tsx` does;
  3. keep every assertion.

### Disputed note 2 — the ruled ranking makes some outcomes I think you should look at (not implemented)

Evidence, from the ruled rule set as implemented:
1. **Row 6 tilts the score toward W.** It is n/a for every native candidate, so a native path can never earn that
   point. In worked example 2 that decides the result: a 50 Hz web path (row 7 at fail, about a quarter of the
   200 Hz target) beats a 90 Hz AppKit path that passes everything else.
2. **A path without pressure can win over one with pressure.** In the reviewer's case, W has 7 passes with pressure
   at fail, under 16 levels, and is selected over R, which delivers pressure (6 passes). The outcome is "pressure
   unsupported on this platform" although a measured path supports it.
3. **No automatic no-go remains.** A log where every row fails on every path still ends in "go-with-conditions" with
   a list of unsupported features. Decision 7's both-platforms escalation can then only come from a human reading
   that list.

Options for your ruling, each a few lines in `aggregate` plus the matching ADR text:
- (a) score by the share of applicable rows at pass, so n/a is not a penalty;
- (b) give precedence to the rows that make a tablet usable (1, 2 and 7), then rank by passes;
- (c) keep the current ranking and add a "no-go" outcome when the selected candidate fails row 2 or row 7;
- (d) keep it as is.

I recommend (a) together with (c). Nothing of this is implemented.

## Addendum 2: final ranking ruling (disqualification, then share)
