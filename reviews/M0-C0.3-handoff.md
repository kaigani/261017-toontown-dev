### Estimators

Stated in the analyzer and the page:
- **frame interval** = consecutive rAF timestamps during pan/zoom; **fps** = (n−1)·1000/(last−first);
- **display refresh** = *mean* idle rAF interval. The median would be biased by WebKit's 1 ms timestamp rounding;
- **long frame** = interval > 1.5 × refresh;
- **target met** = fps ≥ 57;
- **CPU frame** = JS update + record + submit;
- **GPU frame** (WebGPU only) = submit → `onSubmittedWorkDone` (includes queueing behind presentation);
- **transfer** = `performance.now()` around one binary invoke (host encode + IPC + envelope check);
- **IPC-per-frame ceiling** = 1000 / p50 transfer: the frame rate if the whole list crossed IPC every frame;
- percentiles are nearest-rank.

What these numbers show:
- **Target: met.** 60 fps pan/zoom at 100 000 paths on this machine, with both WebGPU and WebGL2 (also with
  WebGL2 forced on its own). There were no long frames in ~600 per run. The
  display caps the rate. CPU frame time is p95 ≤ 0.3 ms and max ≤ 1.6 ms. WebGPU GPU time for 100 k paths is
  p50 8.5 ms, about 2× headroom under 16.7 ms.
- **IPC.** Binary `Response` transfers run at **128–172 MB/s** on WebView2: 8.7 MB in p50 50–57 ms. Sending the whole
  100 k list every frame would cap at ~17–20 fps, and the brush scene at ~15 fps, before decode (85–250 ms).
  Rust encoding itself is only ~2 ms for 8.7 MB. **Implication for C0.4/M4:** keep geometry resident on the GPU
  side, and send per-frame deltas such as transforms (groups1k's whole list is 2 MB / ~14 ms) or dirty regions.
  Re-sending the full list per frame does not reach 60 fps.
- **Decode** (flatten + earcut, JS, single thread): 85–100 ms for 100 k strokes and 225–245 ms for 10 k brush
  polygons. Acceptable for loading a drawing, not per frame. The GPU geometry is 90.6 MB for paths100k (2.83 M
  stroke segments) and 30.2 MB for brush10k (1.06 M triangles).

### Fallbacks (plan §2.3), measured as far as cheap

Plan §2.3 names one fallback for canvas throughput, a native `wgpu` child surface. I measured or estimated two:
- **(a) Host-side CPU raster, bitmap per frame over IPC** (the cheapest alternative to build). Per frame this costs
  CPU raster time plus pixel transfer plus texture upload:
  - **pixel transfer + upload**, measured: 1904×757 (5.77 MB) takes **p50 36–39 ms + 0.3–1.6 ms upload** on this
    machine. That alone caps the rate at ~25 fps;
  - **CPU raster**, from `c03_raster --canvas-bench`: `vello_cpu` renders the same scenes at the starting camera in
    the times below.

    | Scene | 1280×720, 1 thread / 3 threads | 1920×1080, 1 thread / 3 threads |
    |---|---|---|
    | paths100k | 559 / 197 ms | 1152 / 398 ms |
    | groups1k | 77 / 26 ms | 82 / 30 ms |
    | brush10k | 75 / 35 ms | 85 / 34 ms |

    The CI runners are similar or slower (`raster-comparison.md`).

  **Not viable for 60 fps** at these scene sizes (paths100k ≈ 2–4 fps).
- **(b) Native `wgpu` child surface: estimate only, not built** (as ruled). It would remove the IPC copy
  (≈150 MB/s) and the JS decode, keeping tessellation and upload in Rust. The per-frame GPU work would be of the
  same order as WebGPU's measured 4–9 ms (p50) on this GPU. The real costs would be in the windowing and
  composition with WebView2/WKWebView (a child HWND/NSView above or below the webview, input routing, resize
  synchronisation), which this spike did not measure. Given (a) and the 60 fps result in the webview, no fallback
  is needed on this machine.

## Part B — deterministic CPU rasteriser

### What was built

| Item | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fixtures | `crates/render/examples/c03_raster/scene.rs` | **23 scenes** (22 at 128×128 + one 512×512 performance scene). They cover: cubic curves; thin strokes (0.1–1.5 px); AA edges at 64 directions; overlapping translucent fills; a 20-layer alpha stack; linear, radial and rotated gradients; clip and nested clips; a glyph-like outline with a counter (non-zero and even-odd); degenerate paths (zero-area, collinear, zero-length, empty); a self-intersecting star (both rules); coincident and abutting edges; a path cancelled by its reverse; extreme coordinates (±1e6–1e7, 1e-3 px); sub-pixel translations (k/8 px); non-integer scales (0.37, 0.5, 1.5, 2.71); stroked cubics; 500 random translucent triangles; a hairline grid. **Exact arithmetic only:** angles come from the rational circle parametrisation and transforms are literal; no libm. |
| In-house rasteriser `ttraster` | `ttraster.rs` (487 lines, no dependencies, no `unsafe`) | Exact-area signed-area accumulation (font-rs family): f64 geometry, f32 accumulation, **8-bit coverage**, non-zero = min(\|a\|, 1), even-odd = triangle wave. Single-threaded, every sum in a fixed order. Strokes are polygonised (quads, bevel joins, butt caps) and filled non-zero. Compositing is premultiplied source-over **in sRGB-encoded space**, with integer `div255` rounding. **Decision: sRGB-space blending**, because linear-light needs `powf` (libm) or a table, and sRGB space is what canvas and `vello_cpu`'s u8 pipeline do. |
| `vello_cpu` 0.3.0 | `vello.rs` | `std` + `u8_pipeline` (+ `multithreading`), no text/PNG features. Three configurations: `Level::fallback()` (scalar; needs `fearless_simd/force_support_fallback`), the detected SIMD level, and the detected level with 3 worker threads. |
| Driver | `main.rs` | Renders each scene `--repeat` times (hashes must agree across repeats). Writes PNGs and SHA-256 of the raw premultiplied RGBA8 (with a canonical header) and of the PNG file. `--write-hashes` / `--check` against the committed file; `--report` JSON with all timings; `--canvas-bench`. |
| Committed hashes | `crates/render/examples/c03_raster/hashes.json` | 23 scenes × 4 configurations = 92 entries (pixels + PNG). |
| CI | `ci.yml` job `raster determinism` (Windows, macOS, Linux) | Two separate processes per OS with `--check`; **the job fails on any difference**. Artifacts are PNGs plus reports. |
| Comparison | `scripts/compare-raster-reports.py` | Recomputes hash identity and per-scene medians from the reports' raw data. Output: `reviews/evidence/M0-C0.3/raster-comparison.md`. |

### Measured

- **Hash stability: 92 / 92 identical everywhere.** That holds for every configuration on:
  - this machine: release ×3 processes, plus a debug build;
  - Windows x86-64 (AVX2), macOS arm64 (NEON) and Linux x86-64 (AVX2) on CI run 39121780905, two processes each.

  This includes `vello_cpu` with platform SIMD (AVX2 vs NEON) and with 3 threads. Reproduce with
  `python scripts/compare-raster-reports.py crates/render/examples/c03_raster/hashes.json reviews/evidence/M0-C0.3/raster-report-*.json`.
- **Speed** (median ms, this machine):

  | Scene | `ttraster` | `vello_cpu` scalar | `vello_cpu` SIMD | `vello_cpu` 3 threads |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 128×128 scenes | 0.03–0.38 | 0.03–0.30 | 0.01–0.20 | 0.10–0.35 |
  | 2 000 cubics at 512×512 | 17.1 | 6.5 | 5.0 | 2.7 |

  On this machine and the CI runners, `ttraster` is ≈2.5–3.1× slower than scalar `vello_cpu` on the performance
  scene. `ttraster` is
  unoptimised: a scalar per-pixel paint evaluation over each item's bounding box.
- **Agreement between the rasterisers** (informational; they need not be equal): max channel difference vs
  `vello_cpu` scalar is 0–3 on the edge-angle, thin-stroke, alpha-stack, linear and rotated gradient, star,
  coincident-edge, extreme-coordinate and hairline scenes. It is 15–132 on scenes whose curve flattening, radial
  gradient, clips or stroke joins differ (cubic blobs 62, radial gradient 48, clips 43/68, stroked curves 132;
  `ttrasterVsVelloFallback` in the reports). Visual check: side-by-side contact sheet, identical
  shapes.
- **Dependency weight and safety** (`reviews/evidence/M0-C0.3/vello-dependency-audit.txt`):
  - `vello_cpu` with `multithreading` pulls in 34 crates, ≈338 k lines of Rust, all MIT/Apache-2.0/Zlib (Unicode-3.0
    for `unicode-ident`; `cargo deny` passes);
  - `vello_cpu` and `vello_common` have **0** `unsafe` lines themselves. Their dependencies have 1 728 lines
    mentioning `unsafe` (`hashbrown`, `bytemuck`, `crossbeam-*`, `fearless_simd`, `rayon`…);
  - `ttraster`: 0 dependencies, 0 `unsafe`, 487 lines.
- **Floating point:**
  - No `fast-math` anywhere (Rust has none). Rust never contracts `a*b+c` into FMA on its own. Both rasterisers
    are single-threaded in their summation order, except `vello_cpu_mt3`, whose output is still identical;
  - `fearless_simd`'s AVX2 level includes FMA instructions, and NEON code can use fused operations. They produced
    identical u8 results here;
  - **libm:** `ttraster` makes no libm calls. `vello_common` uses `powf` in curve flattening (`flatten_simd.rs:671`,
    subdivision count `err_div.powf(1/6).ceil()`) and in blurs. `kurbo`/`color` use `atan2`, `hypot`, `powf`, `cbrt`
    and `sin_cos` on some paths;
  - so `vello_cpu`'s determinism across platforms rests on libm agreeing at the points where results are rounded.
    That held on these three platforms but is not guaranteed.

## Known gaps

1. **No macOS hardware measurement**: the macOS runner has a paravirtual GPU and no WebGPU adapter. Pending user.
2. **The spike renderer is not a product renderer.**
   - Cubics are flattened once at a fixed world tolerance, so deep zoom shows facets.
   - Strokes are per-segment quads: joins overlap, so translucent strokes double-blend at joins, and there are no
     real caps or joins.
   - Draw order is all fills, then all strokes.
   - There is no clipping, gradients or effects on the GPU path.
   - Decode runs on the main thread.
   - These choices favour throughput measurement; M4 must preserve painter's order and correct stroking.
3. **GPU timing on WebGL2 is not measured** (no timer query used); fps and CPU time are. WebGPU's GPU time includes
   queueing.
4. **IPC-per-frame streaming was measured as sequential transfers**, not as a streamed animation. The ceiling is an
   upper bound that excludes decode and draw.
5. **Determinism coverage is limited to:**
   - x86-64 AVX2 and arm64 NEON. AVX-512 (`Level::Avx512`), SSE4.2-only CPUs and other libm versions are untested;
   - `vello_cpu`'s u8 pipeline only (the f32 `OptimizeQuality` pipeline is not enabled);
   - this exact toolchain and lockfile.
6. The pixel-frame fallback used a synthetic pattern (the IPC cost doesn't depend on content); real raster plus
   transfer is an estimate (sum), not an end-to-end run.
7. The spike commands are registered in release builds (gated by the launch flag, like C0.2's); remove or dev-gate
   them with the other M0 diagnostics.

## Open questions

1. **Fallbacks:** plan §2.3 names one canvas fallback (native `wgpu` surface). I additionally measured the host-raster
   bitmap path as the cheap alternative. Is that the second "fallback" the C0.3 ruling meant, or should C0.4 consider
   another?
2. **Render-list transport for M4:** the measurements favour a GPU-resident scene with per-frame deltas over
   re-sending render lists (WebView2 IPC ≈150 MB/s). C0.4 should record this as an architecture input. Not decided
   here.
3. **Rasteriser choice (C0.4 ADR):** both rasterisers are hash-stable across the three CI platforms.
   - `vello_cpu`: 2.5–6× faster, with gradients, clips, filters and strokes built in; the cost is a 34-crate
     dependency tree and libm reliance on a few rounding paths.
   - `ttraster`: 487 dependency-free lines, but missing features and speed.

   The evidence is in this handoff; the decision belongs to C0.4.
