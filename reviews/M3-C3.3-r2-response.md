## 4. ADR-0020 decision 11 (as recorded)

> **Recovery contract** (rulings C33-R1-08, C33-R2-01, C33-R2-02). Recovery acts only on files the product can prove it
> owns. *Exact names:* a job's token is exactly 12 lowercase hexadecimal characters; its files are
> `.<target>.tt-partial-<token>` and `.<target>.tt-backup-<token>`, each `<target>` a file name the job's own pattern
> and range produce for the directory. *Owned sidecar:* before its first staged write a job writes
> `.tt-partial-<token>.job` (kind, version, token, jobId, head, productVersion, host, pid, published, targets, and the
> derived staged and backup names), removed last. *Who recovers what:* only valid sidecars are read and only the files
> they list are acted on; unlisted files, malformed tokens, marker-like names and invalid sidecars are never touched
> and never reported; the next job recovers sidecars whose job is not alive (this process, token no longer live; another
> local process known to have exited); another machine's or an unknown process's job is left alone; a cancelled or
> failed job recovers its own sidecar only. *What recovery does:* listed staged files are removed; a listed backup is
> deleted when the sidecar is `published`, otherwise its original goes back onto the target; the sidecar is removed only
> when nothing failed. *Receipts:* `render.staleStagingRemoved` in the next job's warnings, `render.stagingDiscarded` in
> a cancelled or failed job's record (with the typed error when failed), readable through `job.list` and `job.wait`.

Decision 10 now records rollback failures (`export.rollbackIncomplete`, kept backups and sidecar, retry by the next job)
and the `published` mark.
