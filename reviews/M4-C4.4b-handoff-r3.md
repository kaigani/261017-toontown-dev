## 4. The lock (rulings D-83 items 1-2, D-84)

- **Design note** `reviews/M4-design-lock-takeover.md`: the round 2 schedules in §1 (zero owners by creation; no owner
  on Windows), steps C1 (create; give-up removes nothing), C2 (identity under the protection) and A4' (rename aside),
  §4.1 (contention, not "not writable"; "held" only when true; the 2 s rounds and why), and why each schedule cannot
  happen now (§5), including Windows delete pending.
- **Built after the hold:** everything compiled as written. One Windows correction: `std::fs::remove_file` uses POSIX
  delete semantics on NTFS, so the name is freed at once even while a handle is open; the zero-owner and two-owner
  schedules therefore run on Windows too, and the delete-pending test now stages a legacy delete-on-close deletion (as a
  proof's handle deletes), probed first by a small script: once that handle closes while the holder's is open, opens
  and creations fail with error 5 until the holder closes. Its expectation follows D-84: the paused holder, whose arbiter
  was deleted, closes its first publication, decides again and owns the lock, reporting the stale lock it took over
  (`50e9ed28`).
- **Tests** (`c44b_lock_takeover.rs`, 11, all passing on Windows; CI runs them on both systems): the releasing arbiter; the abandoned arbiter proven once; the zero-owner and
  two-owner schedules (both systems; W's `Acquired` before D resumes); Windows legacy delete pending; creation before
  the protection (C44B-R2S-01's schedule); a creator's give-up racing a prover (Unix); a participant ending at each of
  11 pause points (a child process); the reviewer's foreign-reader reproduction; a reader refusing deletion (Windows);
  a folder that cannot be written (Unix); 40 randomised trials (seed printed, `TT_LOCK_SEED`).
- **Receipts** (`reviews/evidence/M4-C4.4b-r3/lock-loops-idle.json`, `lock-loops-busy.json`): the randomised test
  (`many_contenders_with_jitter_leave_exactly_one_owner_whose_handle_stays_valid`, 40 trials of 6 to 12 contenders
