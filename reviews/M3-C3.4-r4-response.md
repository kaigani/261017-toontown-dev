## 2. O-6: the cumulative stall

The code already gives the guarantee, so no per-request question budget is added. ADR-0020 decision 8 now states it:
- **One question:** a question never holds the document thread longer than one bound.
- **Unanswered questions:** each costs one bound, and the link latches after the first, so they add at most one bound
  per request, whatever its step count.
- **Answered questions:** these don't latch. All questions together hold the document thread for at most the
  request's own watchdog budget plus one bound. At its deadline the caller stops answering: it marks its token
  (timeout) and returns, dropping its end of the link. A question in flight then ends at once (the caller is gone) or
  at its bound. Every later boundary sees the marked token and asks nothing, and the worker stops at its next boundary
  or work-account check.

The ADR's shorthand "one request stalls the document thread by at most one bound" is replaced by "one bound per
unanswered question, latched after the first" and the cumulative statement above.

Builder test `c34_round4::answers_just_inside_the_bound_hold_the_thread_at_most_the_budget_plus_one_bound`:
- **Setup:** a 200-step sequential batch of creates, a 1500 ms budget and a 250 ms bound. A probe answers each new
  question after 200 ms (0.8 × the bound), and the caller's polls between questions return at once.
- **Measured locally:** 8 questions answered; the request ended by its watchdog (`command.timeout`, no `reason`) after
  1615 ms, against the 2000 ms limit of budget + 2 × bound. Two steps committed, one revision each; the rest changed
  nothing, and `doc.validate` reports no error. An unrelated query issued straight afterwards was served in 1.1 ms.

## 2a. A CI finding at the first push (run 43199511075), fixed

- **Failure:** macOS `check`: the reviewer's round-1 `c34_r1_contract::sequential_wait_timeout_retains_job_identity`
  (body unchanged) runs a plain sequential batch with a 500 ms budget, whose waited job step is bounded by the budget
  minus a tenth. The batch answered with no steps, so the caller's watchdog had answered first.
- **Cause:** the batch measured its remaining budget from when it began on the document thread, but its caller's
  deadline runs from when the request was enqueued. Queueing time therefore came out of the 50 ms margin left for the
  rest of the batch and the reply. A loaded runner exceeded it. No probe or link is involved, and this round's changes
  do not touch the path.
- **Fix:** a caller's request carries its enqueue instant, and the caller's deadline is that instant plus the budget.
  A sequential batch's waited job step and `job.wait` step are bounded from it. ADR-0020's batch-wait exception says
  so.
- **Evidence:** builder `c34_round4::a_queued_batch_wait_is_bounded_from_its_callers_enqueue_not_from_its_start`. A
  waited batch queued behind another for most of its 1000 ms budget answers with its steps (`job.waitTimeout` and the
  `jobId`) within its budget. Without the fix, its caller times out with no steps, every time.

- **A second CI finding (run 43207004967), in this round's own builder test.** On macOS `check`, the O-6 test's
  batch finished all its steps instead of ending by its watchdog.
  - **Likely cause:** the probe's 200 ms sleep overshot past the 250 ms bound on that runner, so the answer was late
    and the link latched, as C34-R4-02 requires. The trial then no longer tested its premise that every answer is
    inside the bound. The failing trial printed no timings, so this is inferred rather than shown.
  - **Fix:** the test keeps 0.8 × the bound. The probe times its own answers. A trial in which an answer overran 0.9 ×
    the bound, or a finished batch that asked fewer than its 1 + 3 × 200 questions (both signs that the link latched),
    is retried, up to five times. A trial with every answer inside the bound must meet every assertion. The test fails
    if no trial in five keeps every answer inside the bound. Each attempt prints its timings. Product code is
    unchanged.
